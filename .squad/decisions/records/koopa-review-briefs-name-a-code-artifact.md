### 2026-09-21: A review brief names a code artifact, and the artifacts are enumerated per feature — design documents are a coordination surface, not a defect surface
**By:** Koopa (Retrospective Analyst)

**What:** Every review pass scheduled on a feature — architecture re-review, acceptance, security, QA — gets a brief naming the **code artifact** its reviewer is to read. The phase plan enumerates that feature's truth-holding artifacts once, up front, and assigns one reviewer per artifact rather than one per layer. Reviewing the design documents remains worth doing for internal consistency; it is not to be counted as defect discovery, and a pass whose brief names only documents should be understood as covering nothing about the system.

**Why:**

- **Measured on the mystery-entity-linking feature (10 commits, `c38b02a..68ea8c5`): roughly fifty substantive findings came from reading code or running mutants, and four from reading documents against each other.** The four were internal-consistency defects (`§6.1` vs `§5.4` disagreeing about `MonsterDraft`'s baselines; a ruling bound to the wrong progress indicator; a stale scope line; two follow-up lists drifting). None of them was a fact about the system.
- **The decisive case is Bowser's.** Both design documents asserted that changing a minion's parent monster was "just a field edit" needing no special treatment — Yoshi's §6.4 and Rosalina's §6. It never was: `UpsertMinionRequest` carries no `MonsterId` and `MinionService.UpdateAsync` never sets one, so the described call returns 200 and silently does nothing. It survived to rev 7 and was caught only because Yoshi wrote the re-review's brief as *"read the code, not the plan."* **The two documents agreed with each other, which is exactly why document review could not find it. Agreement between documents is not evidence.** Cost of the correction: one unplanned commit (`ef28fd5`), a new backend verb with 14 tests, and a round-trip to the stakeholder.
- **Peach reached the same conclusion independently, three times, and stated it twice in the same words.** Phase 4: *"Read the store, not just the two design docs. All three gaps came from `mystery-create.store.ts` and `mystery-create.html`. Neither design doc was wrong; both were silent, and silence is invisible from inside the docs."* Phase 5b: *"Design docs describe the new behaviour; the code holds the old invariant, and the gap between them is where every finding has come from."* Her highest-yield file on all three passes was `core/models.ts`, which nothing in an acceptance-review brief told her to open — **the yield depended on one person choosing a reading surface nobody assigned her.**
- **The three-stage re-review (architect → backend → frontend) worked, but not by its stated mechanism.** What varied between stages was not depth or layer but *the artifact each reviewer opens by habit* — Bowser opens `ApiContracts.cs`, Peach opens `core/models.ts` and the store, Luigi greps his own store for consumers, Toad writes the repro. Proof that ordering was not the mechanism: a fourth pass (Peach's Phase 5 acceptance) found **five** further defects in a document that had already been through all three stages. If the chain worked by depth, a later pass should find less.
- **The corollary that makes this cheap:** for that feature the truth-holding artifacts were four — the C# request records, `core/models.ts`, `mystery-create.store.ts`, and a running repro. Enumerating them takes minutes at phase-plan time. Three stages happened to cover three of the four; the fourth arrived last and found the two most dangerous defects in the feature (a failed edit-mode hydration that silently unlinked every monster from the mystery, and a permanently stuck phase).

**Boundary.** This does not devalue the design documents. 161KB of architecture and 141KB of UX are why seven contributors worked one feature in nineteen hours of wall clock without colliding, and why nineteen decisions were taken across four stakeholder rounds without anything being re-litigated. Their value is coordination and record. The change is only to stop briefing reviewers *at* them and counting the result as coverage.

**Companion standing instruction, Peach's, worth adopting verbatim:** *"Check the payload, not just the entity. Design docs describe entities; contracts describe payloads, and that is where the silence concentrates."*
